# Brain to happiness gauge — source notes

- Source: [“Motion Graphic for a reel (Style VOX)”](https://www.youtube.com/shorts/gtxHSafv2Lk), by Mohamed A. (watermark in the clip).
- Reference duration: about 14.65 seconds, 360 × 640 source available for frame study.
- Spoken narration: “Dopamine is the brain chemical associated with pleasure and reward. Its activity happens in specific regions of the brain, and it influences how much enjoyment you feel.” This describes the topic; it is not an explicit motion-design instruction.

## Visible construction

1. 0–2 s: A monochrome anatomical brain illustration enters and grows large over a warm, grainy paper background with faint molecular diagrams.
2. 2–5 s: A violet ring and translucent fill isolate one brain region. A thin line extends to a blue serif “Dopamine” label and small definition beneath it.
3. 5–7 s: The labeled diagram recedes and the brain shrinks toward the lower center.
4. 7–10 s: The same brain becomes the anchor of a semicircular “happiness” gauge. The gauge grows around it, with dotted yellow edging, warm red glow, a sad face at the low end and a gradient bar above.
5. 10–14.6 s: Warm coloring transitions to green as the brain and highlighted area enlarge and pulse; the arc eventually extends beyond the frame. The movement follows the narration’s shift from identification of dopamine to the amount of enjoyment felt.

## Transferable principle (visual inference)

When an explanation moves from **where or what something is** to **what it changes**, preserve the same visual object as an anchor and transform the surrounding diagram into a measure of the consequence. Here the brain first carries a localized label, then becomes the center of a happiness scale. Color and size changes make the abstract change readable before a viewer can parse small text. This is an inference from this example, not a spoken instruction or an official Vox rule.
